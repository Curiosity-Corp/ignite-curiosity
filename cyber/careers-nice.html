Defend the future: find your place in cyber

Cybersecurity is not one job. It is hundreds of ways to protect people, and many of them do not need a computer science degree. The hard part for a newcomer is knowing which door to knock on. The U.S. has a shared map for that: the NICE Framework, a common language for cyber work. This page is the D: Defend the future step of SHIELD 2040. You will learn how the map works, use free career tools to explore it, and match popular roles to free training on this site and to free credentials. Wherever the facts about a program are uncertain, we say so.

The NICE Framework explained

NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1, Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity (NICE Framework) was published in November 2020. It sets out the structure, and NIST maintains the current content separately as "NICE Framework Components" so it can be updated without rewriting the publication. When we checked in October 2026, NICCS and the NIST Resource Center both listed Components version 2.2.0 as current. (NIST's page date line looked ambiguous, so always confirm on the Current Versions page.) The Framework has four building blocks:

Work Role Categories

Five broad groups of cyber work: Oversight and Governance (OG), Design and Development (DD), Implementation and Operation (IO), Protection and Defense (PD) and Investigation (IN).

Work Roles

Groupings of work someone is responsible for, such as Incident Response or Cybersecurity Policy and Planning. NICE is clear that work roles are not job titles; one job may combine several, and one role may appear under many titles.

Competency Areas

Clusters of related knowledge and skills for a topic. Version 2.2.0 lists 11: Access Controls, Artificial Intelligence (AI) Security, Asset Management, Cloud Security, Communications Security, Cryptography, Cyber Resiliency, DevSecOps, Operating Systems (OS) Security, Operational Technology (OT) Security and Supply Chain Security.

TKS statements

The smallest pieces: Task statements (what is done), Knowledge statements (what you must know) and Skill statements (what you must be able to do). Everything else is built from them.

Browse them yourself: work role categories, work role search, competency areas and TKS search on NICCS. The Components are also free to download as a spreadsheet or JSON from the NICE Framework Resource Center. A companion report, NISTIR 8355, explains how competency areas were developed.

A note about the ISC2 "Certified in Cybersecurity" free offer

ISC2's One Million Certified in Cybersecurity program closed to new participants on May 20, 2026, according to ISC2's own page. Only people who already held an unexpired exam code can still sit the free exam, by December 31, 2026. Everyone else pays the standard exam fee (US $199) plus an annual maintenance fee. We therefore do not include it in our $0 path.

Programs, apprenticeships and funding

These are not courses. They are routes that can fund your learning or pay you while you learn. Eligibility and deadlines change, so confirm details on each official page.

CyberCorps: Scholarship for Service (SFS)

A National Science Foundation program, managed with OPM and CISA. It funds cybersecurity-related study (NICCS says fields include AI, quantum computing and aerospace) at participating colleges that are NCAE-C schools. Per NICCS: tuition and fees for up to three years, a yearly stipend ($27,000 undergraduate, $37,000 graduate) and a $6,000 professional allowance. In return you work for the government in a cyber role for a period equal to your scholarship. Cost: Funded scholarship, with a service obligation · Level: college undergraduate or graduate

Visit SFS NICCS scholarships page

NSA NCAE-C: National Centers of Academic Excellence in Cybersecurity

A designation for colleges and universities with strong cybersecurity programs. The NCAE-C community site offers an Institution Map to find designated schools near you, a Career Explorer, and a students page. Using the map and tools is free; tuition at a school is not, though SFS (above) is tied to these schools. Check each school for two-year options. The community site lists four communities of practice: Cyber Defense, Cyber Operations, Cyber Research and Artificial Intelligence. Cost: Free tools; school costs vary

NCAE-C community Institution Map

Apprenticeship.gov: cybersecurity apprenticeships

Registered apprenticeships combine paid on-the-job work with related instruction (confirm pay and terms with each sponsor). Apprenticeship.gov's Technology industry page lists Cybersecurity Support Technician among high-demand tech occupations, and the Department of Labor publishes a cybersecurity apprenticeship fact sheet. NICCS also points to the DHS Cybersecurity Apprenticeship Program and an apprenticeship finder on its internships and apprenticeships page. Cost: Typically paid work; verify per program

NICE K12: cybersecurity for younger learners

NIST's NICE program supports the NICE K12 Cybersecurity Education Conference (hosted by iKeepSafe); NIST currently lists the 12th annual event for March 7-9, 2027 in New Orleans, moved from December 2026. NICCS has a page for K-12 teachers and a competitions and games page. Learners can also try picoCTF (free) from our Hands-On page. Cost: Free resources; conference fees not verified · Level: ages 11-18 and teachers

Community college pathways

Two-year colleges are a low-cost route into cyber. Use the NCAE-C Institution Map above to find designated schools, CyberSeek to see which credentials employers in your area ask for, and Apprenticeship.gov to see if a college partners with an employer. Ask each school about its cybersecurity certificate or associate degree, financial aid and transfer agreements. We did not verify any single college program, so treat this as a search method, not an endorsement. Cost: College costs vary; financial aid may apply

For Curiosity Coaches

Discussion prompts

  • "Which of the five NICE categories sounds most like something you already enjoy: protecting, building, investigating, running things or setting rules?"
  • "Pick a work role. What would a normal day look like? What would make it hard?"
  • "What is the difference between a work role and a job title? Why might that help you?"
  • "If you could earn only one free badge this month, which would help you most, and why?"

A 4-week plan (one 45-minute session per week)

Week 1: explore the five categories with the NICCS Career Pathways Roadmap and pick three roles that look interesting. Week 2: use CyberSeek's Career Pathway on those three roles and compare skills, salary and openings. Week 3: start one free credential (IBM Cybersecurity Fundamentals for most learners) and pick one hands-on lab from our Hands-On page. Week 4: the learner writes a one-page plan: target role, three competency areas, two free courses, one program to look into, and one question for a professional.

Age and level fit

Ages 11-14: NICCS pages for K-12, picoCTF, role-matching games; no accounts beyond what a parent approves. Ages 15-18: CyberSeek, IBM SkillsBuild, NCAE-C map, SFS awareness. Career-changers: work role search, CISA Learning, apprenticeships and the mapping tool to translate past experience. Please keep personal data off third-party sites unless the learner (and a guardian, for minors) agrees.

Cyber Careers FAQ

Do I need a degree to work in cybersecurity?

Not always. Many roles value skills and hands-on proof, and apprenticeships, certificates and community colleges are real routes in. Some government and research roles do prefer degrees, and SFS funds college study. CyberSeek shows the education level employers ask for by role.

What is a TKS statement?

TKS stands for Task, Knowledge and Skill. A Task says what is done, a Knowledge statement says what you must know, and a Skill statement says what you must be able to do. NICE builds work roles and competency areas out of them.

Which NICE version is current?

NICCS and the NIST Resource Center list NICE Framework Components v2.2.0 (we checked in October 2026). The structure comes from SP 800-181 Rev. 1 (2020); the components are updated separately, so confirm on the NIST Current Versions page.

Is there a free certification I can start today?

Yes, free badges and achievements: IBM SkillsBuild Cybersecurity Fundamentals, Microsoft Learn AI security modules and the Linux Foundation LFD121 badge. The ISC2 free CC offer has closed to new participants, and the big paid exams are optional later.

Where do I start if I am 14?

Try picoCTF, the NICCS pages for K-12, and our Security Foundations page. Ask a parent or coach before creating any account. Hands-on practice should stay inside games and labs built for it.

Track your progress

Keep a "career notebook": the three roles you are curious about, one fact you learned about each, and the next free step. Review it with your coach every month.
