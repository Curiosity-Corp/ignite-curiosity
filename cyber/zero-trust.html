Never trust, always verify: Identity & Zero Trust

For decades, security worked like a castle: a strong wall around the network, and anyone inside was trusted. Then people started working from home, apps moved to the cloud and attackers learned to steal passwords. Zero trust flips the idea. Nobody gets trusted just because of where they are on a network. Every request is checked: who is asking, from what device, for which resource, right now. In 2026 identity is the front door of almost every system, and passkeys and phishing-resistant sign-in are replacing passwords. NIST's published guidance (SP 800-207, SP 1800-35 and the new SP 800-63-4) and CISA's maturity model all point toward that way of working. We expect it to keep spreading through 2040, but that date is our own forward-looking framing, not a NIST requirement. This page is the I in SHIELD: Identity & zero trust.

The big ideas, in plain English

All links are official or vendor-neutral where possible. Hours are our estimates.

NIST SP 800-207: Zero Trust Architecture

Published in August 2020 and still the reference definition. It says zero trust moves defenses away from static network perimeters and toward users, assets and resources, and that no trust is granted implicitly because of network location or ownership. Authentication and authorization for both the person and the device happen before a session to a resource starts. NIST's companion papers add cloud-native and implementation detail. Provider: NIST. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 3 hours (estimate) · Level: Beginner to Intermediate.

SP 800-207 SP 800-207A

NIST SP 1800-35: Implementing a Zero Trust Architecture

From NIST's National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence (NCCoE). The final high-level document (June 10, 2025) describes 19 example zero-trust implementations built with 24 collaborators, so you can see how real products fit together. The NCCoE project page links the how-to guides. Provider: NIST NCCoE. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 3 hours for the high-level document (estimate) · Level: Intermediate.

SP 1800-35 NCCoE project page

CISA Zero Trust Maturity Model, Version 2.0

A friendly yardstick built to line up with OMB memo M-22-09, the U.S. federal zero-trust strategy. It has five pillars (Identity, Devices, Networks, Applications and Workloads, Data), three cross-cutting capabilities (Visibility and Analytics, Automation and Orchestration, Governance) and four stages: Traditional, Initial, Advanced and Optimal. You do not need to be a government agency to use it as a checklist. Provider: CISA. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 2 hours (estimate) · Level: Beginner to Intermediate.

Maturity Model page Version 2.0 (PDF) OMB M-22-09

NIST SP 800-63-4: Digital Identity Guidelines

Revision 4 is final (July 2025) and replaces SP 800-63-3. It comes in four volumes: the overview (63-4), Identity Proofing and Enrollment (63A-4), Authentication and Authenticator Management (63B-4) and Federation and Assertions (63C-4). Highlights NIST lists include support for synced authenticators such as synced passkeys, new controls against injection attacks and forged media such as deepfakes, and tougher fraud requirements for identity proofing. Provider: NIST. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 3 hours for the overview and 63B (estimate) · Level: Intermediate.

SP 800-63-4 (NIST CSRC) Read it online Authentication volume (63B-4)

MFA, phishing-resistant sign-in and passkeys

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) asks for more than a password. But a one-time code can still be tricked out of you on a fake website. Phishing-resistant methods, such as passkeys and hardware security keys built on FIDO standards, tie your sign-in to the real website, so a look-alike site gets nothing. CISA publishes a short fact sheet on phishing-resistant MFA, and the FIDO Alliance and the open passkeys.dev site explain how passkeys work. Provider: CISA, FIDO Alliance, passkeys.dev. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 2 hours (estimate) · Level: Beginner.

CISA: phishing-resistant MFA (PDF) passkeys.dev FIDO Alliance design guidelines CISA: turn on MFA

Least privilege, microsegmentation, ZTNA and SASE

Least privilege: every person, device and app gets the smallest set of access it needs. Microsegmentation: split a network into small zones so an intruder who gets into one cannot wander into the others. ZTNA (zero trust network access): instead of a VPN that puts you on the whole network, you are connected only to the specific app you are allowed to use, after your identity and device are checked. SASE (secure access service edge): a way of delivering networking and security, including ZTNA, as a cloud service. Cloudflare's learning paths and the OpenZiti introduction explain these ideas with diagrams. Provider: Cloudflare Learning Paths and OpenZiti (NetFoundry). You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 3 hours (estimate) · Level: Beginner.

OpenZiti: zero trust vs. VPN OpenZiti: zero trust models Cloudflare learning paths

Google BeyondCorp: where the idea went mainstream

Google moved its own workforce off a privileged corporate network and onto identity-aware access. The research papers that describe how are free to read and are still some of the clearest explanations of why and how. Provider: Google Research. You earn: none, this is reading. Cost: Free resource · About 3 hours for two papers (estimate) · Level: Intermediate.

Hands-on free labs

Use your own computer or a school lab, and only on systems you own or have permission to use. Free plans change, so check each provider's current limits before you sign up.

OpenZiti local network

Run a small zero-trust overlay on your own machine with the local quickstart, or use the Docker Compose version if you know Docker. Create an identity, write a policy, and watch an app become reachable only to that identity.

Keycloak identity server

Keycloak is open-source identity and access management. Follow its Docker getting-started guide, create a user and add a second factor.

Cloudflare Zero Trust free plan

Cloudflare says it offers a free plan for zero-trust access. Pair it with the Clientless Access learning path and the setup guide. Check the current user limit and terms on the plans page first.

Tailscale Personal plan

Tailscale lists a free Personal plan (up to 6 users, non-commercial use) for private device-to-device networking with identity-based access rules. Start with the install guide and see its pricing page for current limits.

Passkey playground

Register a passkey on the WebAuthn demo site. Then turn on a passkey or an authenticator app for one of your own real accounts, using CISA's MFA guide.

Maturity scorecard

Print the CISA model pillars. Rate a pretend company from Traditional to Optimal on each one and choose the single change that helps most.

Exercise: the castle versus the checkpoint

Draw your school or club network twice. First as a castle: one wall, everyone inside trusted. Then as a set of checkpoints: for each app (email, files, grades, printers) write who may use it, from which device, and how that is checked. Mark the pillar of the CISA model each checkpoint belongs to. Which drawing would you rather defend against a stolen password?

For Curiosity Coaches

Aim for a 45 to 60 minute session with a drawing or a live demo. Keep the focus on the idea that trust should be earned for each request.

Discussion prompts
  • Why is a password like a key that anyone can copy? What makes a passkey harder to steal?
  • Should the person who runs the printer have the same access as the person who runs payroll? Why or why not?
  • What do you lose if every request gets extra checks? How could a system stay easy to use?
  • Does “never trust” sound unfriendly? How does it protect the people inside the organization?
A 4-week plan
  • Week 1: Microsoft Learn Zero Trust principles, then the castle versus checkpoint exercise.
  • Week 2: CISA fact sheet on phishing-resistant MFA and a passkey demo. Learners secure one of their own accounts.
  • Week 3: Skim NIST SP 800-207 and the CISA maturity model, then complete a maturity scorecard together.
  • Week 4: Pick one lab (OpenZiti, Keycloak or Cloudflare) and present what was protected and how.
Age and level fit
  • Ages 12 to 15: the castle exercise, passkey demo, and MFA setup on their own accounts.
  • Ages 16 and up, and career-changers: add the Microsoft Learn paths and one hands-on lab.
  • IT professionals: go to SP 800-207, SP 1800-35 and the SC-300 learning paths, and plan an OpenZiti or Keycloak pilot.
Careers this connects to

Typical job titles include identity and access management analyst, cloud security engineer, zero-trust architect, network security engineer and security consultant. See NIST's NICE Framework for official work roles and skills.

Zero Trust FAQ

Is the whole path really free?

Yes. Microsoft Learn, the CSA prep kit, OpenZiti docs, passkey guides and CISA Learning cost $0. The CCZT exam ($175) and the SC-300 exam are optional and paid, and you never need them to finish.

Is zero trust a product I can buy?

No. NIST describes it as an approach and an architecture, not a single product. Products help, but the principle is checking every request.

Are SMS codes enough for MFA?

Better than nothing, but they can be phished or intercepted. For important accounts, choose passkeys or a hardware security key where the service allows it.

Is SP 800-63-4 final?

Yes. NIST's page lists the final version as published on July 31, 2025, replacing SP 800-63-3.

Does NIST require zero trust by 2040?

No. NIST describes zero trust as guidance. “By 2040” is our own outlook for where identity and access are heading, not a NIST deadline.
