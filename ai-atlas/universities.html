Free AI learning from the world’s best AI universities

In 2026, AI is not a niche skill. It is how people write, research, build and decide, and by 2040 it will be as ordinary as spreadsheets are today. The good news is that the organizations building AI also teach it, often for free. This page is our definitive directory: every provider below has a free way in, and every course carries an honest cost label and an honest note on whether it gives a certificate or badge. We checked each link, and where we could not confirm something, we say so. Time estimates are ours unless the provider states them.

What you’ll be able to do

Find the right door

Pick a provider that matches your level, your goal and your budget, in a few minutes, not a few weekends.

Read cost labels honestly

Tell a free certificate from a free course, a free-to-audit course and a paid one before you click.

Earn something you can show

Choose the free badges and certificates that are confirmed, and know which courses simply teach without one.

Start with no experience

Begin with plain-language courses that need no coding and no maths, then step up when you are ready.

Build with real tools

Move into hands-on courses on agents, language models and deep learning when you want to make things.

Guide younger learners

Find free AI curricula for kids, teens and teachers, ready to use in class, at a club or at home.

For Curiosity Coaches

Use this page as a menu. Help each learner pick one provider, one goal and one finish line.

Discussion prompts

  • What is one thing you would like AI to help you do this month, and one thing you would never hand to it?
  • Which provider would you trust to teach you, and why? Does a free badge change how much you trust a course?
  • When a course says “certificate not confirmed”, would you still take it? What would you want to learn from it?
  • How would you check an AI answer before you rely on it?

A 4-week plan (about 3 to 4 hours a week)

  • Week 1, Look around: take Elements of AI Part 1 or Claude 101, and try Teachable Machine together.
  • Week 2, Earn a first credential: IBM SkillsBuild AI Fundamentals, Cisco Introduction to Modern AI or Trailhead’s AI Fundamentals badge.
  • Week 3, Go deeper: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations, or a short DeepLearning.AI course.
  • Week 4, Make and share: build one small thing (a Kaggle notebook, a PartyRock app, a Hugging Face lesson) and present it to your coach.

Age and level fit

  • Ages 8 to 12: Code.org (grades 3 to 5), Teachable Machine and Quick, Draw! with a coach or parent.
  • Ages 12 to 18: Code.org AI Discoveries and AI Foundations, Experience AI, Day of AI.
  • Adults, new to AI: Elements of AI, Claude 101, Google Grow, IBM SkillsBuild, Cisco.
  • Career-changers who code: Hugging Face, fast.ai, Kaggle, DeepLearning.AI, CS50 AI.
  • Educators: Khan Academy, Code.org AI 101, AI Fluency for educators, Grow with Google AI for Educators.

Free AI Universities FAQ

Is everything here really free?

Every provider has a free way in. We label each course honestly: Free certificate, Free badge, Free course, or Free to audit with a paid certificate. Google AI Essentials is paid (about $49 USD a month in the U.S. and Canada after a 7-day trial), so we mark it clearly. DeepLearning.AI certificates need a paid PRO plan.

What does “certificate not confirmed” mean?

We could not see a certificate or badge promise on the official page, so we will not promise one. The learning may still be excellent. Check the course page before you start if a credential matters to you.

Which one should a complete beginner start with?

Try Elements of AI, Claude 101, or Cisco’s Introduction to Modern AI. All three assume no experience.

Where do I get a free badge or certificate fastest?

Claude 101 (certificate of completion), IBM SkillsBuild AI Fundamentals (Credly badge), Cisco Introduction to Modern AI (digital badge) and Salesforce Trailhead’s AI Fundamentals badge are all free according to their pages.

Is this good for kids and teens?

Yes. Start with Code.org, Experience AI and Day of AI. Intel’s AI for Youth is also aimed at ages 8 to 19 through partner schools.

Do these links change?

Providers rename and move courses often (Google Cloud Skills Boost is now Google Skills, for example). Tell your coach if a link is dead and we will update it.

Track your progress

Keep a simple list: provider, course, date finished and the link to your badge or certificate. Share it with your coach.
