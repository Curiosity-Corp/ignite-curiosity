From curious to quantum developer: a free roadmap

The quantum workforce is young, and it needs software developers, researchers, engineers and security specialists. This page covers the T in Q.U.B.I.T., Transition to career. You will see the roles, a free 6 to 12 month roadmap to a junior portfolio, the community programs that welcome beginners and an honest look at what certificates cost. Between now and 2040, post-quantum cryptography is likely to be where many people first meet quantum in their jobs.

Five quantum career roles

Quantum is a team sport. Most teams mix physicists, software engineers, mathematicians and domain experts. These are broad descriptions, not job guarantees, and titles vary by employer.

Quantum software developer

Writes the tools and programs that run on quantum computers: circuits, compilers, simulators and cloud services. Python and Qiskit, Cirq or PennyLane are common starting points.

Algorithm researcher

Designs and analyzes quantum algorithms. Leans on linear algebra, probability, complexity theory and often a graduate degree.

Quantum hardware engineer

Builds and controls the machines: superconducting circuits, trapped ions, photonics, cryogenics and control electronics. Physics and electrical engineering are the usual routes.

Quantum applications and industry specialist

Finds where quantum could help in chemistry, finance, logistics or materials, and tests ideas with domain experts. Strong communication and domain knowledge matter as much as code.

Post-quantum security specialist

Helps organizations move to new cryptography that resists quantum attacks. This role is already in demand, and it does not need a quantum computer. See SHIELD 2040: the future to 2040.

A free 6 to 12 month roadmap

Aim for about 5 to 8 hours a week. Hours and timing are our estimates, so go faster or slower. The goal is a small portfolio you can show, not a perfect score.

Months 1 to 2 · Foundations and math

Python basics if you need them, then the math refreshers and the ideas in Quantum Foundations & the Math. Finish Quantum Country’s essay and IBM Basics of Quantum Information.

Months 3 to 4 · Build circuits

Learn Qiskit (or PennyLane) by following the IBM Quantum tutorials. Build Bell states, Grover and the quantum Fourier transform in a simulator. Put each notebook on GitHub with a short README.

Months 5 to 6 · Real algorithms and hardware

Take IBM Quantum Computing in Practice and the advanced courses on IBM Quantum Learning. Run a small job on a real device through a free IBM Quantum account and write up what the noise did to your result.

Months 7 to 9 · Join the community

Apply to a free summer school, a hackathon or a mentorship program. Join a community Slack or Discord and ask questions. Make your first open-source contribution, such as a documentation fix.

Months 10 to 12 · Portfolio and applications

Finish one capstone project (for example a small variational algorithm), polish your GitHub profile and write a short blog post. Then apply for internships, research projects or junior roles, and add a certificate if an employer asks.

Certifications, with honest costs

Certificates can help, but a portfolio you can explain helps more. Start with the free badges, and only pay for an exam when an employer or sponsor asks.

Free badges: IBM Quantum Learning

Provider: IBM Quantum Learning on Credly. You earn: digital badges for individual courses, such as Basics of Quantum Information, Quantum Computing in Practice and Quantum Business Foundations. IBM says you take an exam at IBM Training after the lessons and Credly emails you to claim the badge; the course pages we checked do not state an exam price, so confirm on the page. Cost: Free course · badge: free if the exam is free (not confirmed) · Add a few hours for the exam (estimate) · Level: Beginner to Intermediate

Cost: Paid exam, up to $200 depending on where you live; an optional practice assessment through Pearson VUE costs $30. This is not part of the $0 path. Check the current price on the official page first. About 30 hours of study (estimate) · Level: Intermediate

Build your portfolio and contribute to open source

Employers hire people who can show working code and clear thinking. Open source is the free way to prove both.

  • One repository per project: a notebook, a short README that says what question you asked, and a plot or histogram of the answer.
  • Start small: fix a typo, improve a tutorial or reproduce a published result. Good first contributions are often documentation.
  • Read the contributing guides: Qiskit, Cirq and PennyLane.
  • Explore the ecosystem: the Qiskit ecosystem lists community projects that welcome contributors.
  • Write about it: a short post on what surprised you shows how you think.

Industry and national context

The US National Quantum Initiative

The National Quantum Initiative Act was signed in 2018 and calls for a coordinated federal program to accelerate quantum research and development. The official site hosts the national strategy, news and a resources section. Our “by 2040” framing is ours: it reflects where these public programs and timelines point, not a government requirement.

National Quantum Initiative quantum.gov resources Strategy

Post-quantum cryptography: your first quantum job

NIST has published post-quantum standards (FIPS 203, 204 and 205), and organizations are planning migrations away from today’s vulnerable algorithms. That work needs people who understand both security and quantum, starting now. Continue on SHIELD 2040: the future to 2040.

The US National Science Foundation lists quantum as a focus area, and many universities publish open lecture notes. Use these to read about funding, research and education programs.

For Curiosity Coaches

Use this page to help a learner turn interest into a plan. Keep it motivating, and be honest that quantum careers are young and competitive.

Discussion prompts
  • Which of the five roles sounds most like you, and what would your first project be?
  • What is the difference between a certificate and a portfolio, and when would an employer care about each?
  • Why might post-quantum security be a quantum job that does not need a quantum computer?
  • What small open-source contribution could you make this month?
A 4-week plan
  • Week 1: choose a role and write a one-paragraph goal. Set up a GitHub account and a free IBM Quantum account.
  • Week 2: complete one Qiskit tutorial and push the notebook with a README.
  • Week 3: read a contributing guide and find one issue or documentation fix to try.
  • Week 4: apply to one community program (or add its next date to a calendar) and present the portfolio to the group.
Age and level fit
  • Teens (about 14 and up): focus on Python, the roadmap’s first months and Quirk or Composer; explore roles without pressure.
  • University students: go for summer schools, hackathons and mentorship, and plan for research experience.
  • Career changers: lean on software or security experience, and consider the post-quantum security path first.

Quantum Careers FAQ

Do I need a PhD to work in quantum?

For research and hardware roles, often yes. For software, tooling, applications and security roles, many teams value a strong portfolio and relevant experience. Requirements vary by employer.

Is the whole roadmap free?

Yes. The courses, tools, community programs and open-source work are all free to start. The only paid item on this page is the optional IBM certification exam, which is not needed to finish the roadmap.

How much does the IBM certification cost?

IBM says registration may cost up to $200 per exam, depending on where you live, with an optional $30 practice assessment through Pearson VUE. Check the official page for your country’s price.

Are the free badges really free?

The IBM Quantum Learning courses are free, and each course page says the badge comes from an exam at IBM Training. We could not confirm the exam price on those pages, so we do not promise it is free.

What if a program has closed?

Many programs run once a year. The 2026 Qiskit Global Summer School and unitaryHACK have finished, so use the roadmap and plan for next year while you build your portfolio.

Track your progress

Keep a portfolio checklist: GitHub profile, three notebooks, one write-up, one community program. Tick off each item with your coach.
